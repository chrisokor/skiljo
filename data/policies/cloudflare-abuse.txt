Section 2.2.1 — Restrictions

"You will not and you have no right to: (a) rent, lease, loan, export, or
sell access to the Services to any third party" and "(b) interfere with,
disrupt, alter, or modify the Services or any part thereof."

Additional prohibited conduct under this section includes: "(e) introduce
software or automated agents or scripts into the Services so as to
produce multiple accounts" and "(j) use the Services to provide a virtual
private network or other similar proxy services."

Section 2.7 — Acceptable Use

"You agree not to, and not to allow third parties to use the Services to:
(a) falsely imply any sponsorship or association with Cloudflare" and
"(c) distribute viruses, worms, time bombs, Trojan horses, or other
malicious code."

Section 8 — Suspension and Termination

"We reserve the right to disable or limit your access to or use of the
Service ('Suspend') or terminate your user account upon receiving any
number of DMCA notifications" and Cloudflare "may at our sole discretion
terminate your user account...at any time, with or without notice for any
reason or no reason at all."

Suspension and termination are both framed as unilateral, discretionary
actions available to Cloudflare — there is no described appeal window or
cure period attached to this section.

Section 4.1 — Recurring Billing

"By providing a Payment Method you are authorizing us to charge your
Payment Method on a monthly, annual, or pay-as-you-go basis."

Section 4.3 — No Refunds, With a Discretionary Exception

"FEES ARE NONREFUNDABLE. YOU WILL BE BILLED IN FULL FOR THE SUBSCRIPTION
TERM IN WHICH YOU CANCEL."

Immediately following that unconditional statement, the same section
carves out discretionary room: Cloudflare "may, in our sole discretion,
provide a refund, discount, or credit...however the provision of Credits
in a specific instance does not entitle you to Credits in the future."

Why This Section Is the Interesting Case

Section 4.3 is internally two-part: a capitalized, absolute non-refund
statement immediately followed by a discretionary carve-out that lets
Cloudflare grant a refund, discount, or credit anyway, while explicitly
disclaiming that any single grant sets a precedent. This is a
single-document version of the policy-vs-practice shape seen across the
two Shopify documents in this corpus (shopify-tos.txt vs.
shopify-help-center.txt) — here the absolute statement and the exception
live in the same clause rather than in two separate documents, which is a
useful contrast for the cross-document contradiction detector: does it
also need to flag intra-document tension, or only inter-document tension?
